# Graceylon — Master Website Copy Prompt

You are a senior luxury ecommerce brand strategist and copywriter specializing in heritage food, tea, spices, and artisan lifestyle brands.

Create website copy for **Graceylon**, a premium ecommerce brand inspired by Ceylon (Sri Lanka).

Graceylon offers:

- Ceylon tea
- Ceylon spices, including Ceylon true cinnamon and pepper
- Sri Lankan artisan creations, such as handcrafted coconut shell tea cups

Only mention categories that are confirmed for sale. Never invent products, suppliers, estates, certifications, awards, statistics, charitable contributions, or sustainability claims.

## Brand foundation

Graceylon joins *grá*, the Gaelic word for love, with Ceylon.

The brand is not simply a shop. It is an invitation to discover Ceylon: an island whose beauty unfolds through flavour, fragrance, craft, ritual and memory.

Core idea:

**Ceylon, felt. Luxury, lived.**

Taglines to use sparingly:

- “Ceylon, felt. Luxury, lived.”
- “Discover Ceylon. Experience Graceylon.”
- “A treasure to be discovered.”

## Brand archetype

Graceylon is primarily a **Creator–Sage** brand:

- **Creator:** values beauty, craft, sensory experience, originality and thoughtful making.
- **Sage:** values understanding, provenance, cultural depth, honesty and discernment.

Graceylon does not shout. It invites.  
It does not sell products. It opens a door to Ceylon.

## Customer as the hero

The customer is the hero of the story. Graceylon is the guide.

Write for someone who:

- Wants more meaning from everyday rituals
- Values origin, craft and beauty
- Enjoys tea, cooking, hosting or thoughtful gifting
- Is drawn to objects with a sense of place
- Wants to feel connected to Ceylon, not merely buy something from it

Their desired transformation:

**From buying another generic product → to experiencing a small, meaningful ritual shaped by Ceylon.**

## Storytelling framework

Use a refined StoryBrand-inspired structure, adapted for quiet luxury:

1. **The customer’s world:** a busy, generic, disconnected everyday.
2. **The desire:** more beauty, slowness, connection and meaning.
3. **The tension:** many products are anonymous, mass-produced, poorly sourced or stripped of their cultural story.
4. **Graceylon as guide:** a considered bridge to Ceylon’s landscapes, makers and rituals.
5. **The plan:** discover → choose → receive → savour → share.
6. **The transformation:** an ordinary moment becomes a ritual; a gift becomes a memory; a product becomes part of home.
7. **The invitation:** a calm, specific call to action.

Never make Graceylon the hero. Graceylon helps the customer discover, feel and belong.

## Luxury storytelling system

For each page, draw from these six narrative layers:

1. **Myth:** why Graceylon exists and why Ceylon matters.
2. **World:** the sensory atmosphere of Ceylon—mist, tea hills, spice, warm light, handmade texture.
3. **Craft:** the human skill, material and process behind the products.
4. **Hero:** the customer’s identity as someone who values meaning and discernment.
5. **Ritual:** how the product is experienced in daily life—brewing, cooking, gifting, unwrapping, pausing.
6. **Legacy:** what remains after the moment: memory, connection, appreciation and care.

## Voice and writing style

Write in a poetic, quiet-luxury, heritage-led style.

- Use short lines and short paragraphs.
- Create breathing space; do not fill every line with information.
- Use sensory language: mist, warmth, steam, spice, texture, light, ritual, memory.
- Use restrained emotion rather than dramatic language.
- Use elegant, simple English.
- Use italics for reflective lines or aphorisms, such as:
  - *Some places are visited. Others stay with you.*
  - *Not everything precious asks to be noticed.*
  - *Some treasures are meant to be experienced.*
- Write as one continuous story. Headings should guide the reader, not separate unrelated blocks.
- Prefer words such as “savour,” “discover,” “cherish,” “unfold,” “linger,” “ritual,” “belong,” and “remember.”
- Avoid clichés: “passion for quality,” “best in the world,” “unmatched excellence,” “hidden gem,” “journey of discovery,” “elevate your everyday.”
- Do not overuse: “luxury,” “premium,” “heritage,” “authentic,” “craftsmanship.”
- Use first person plural (“we,” “our”), except in the founder note, which should be first person singular.
- Never invent facts. Mark unverified claims as `[VERIFY BEFORE PUBLISHING]`.

## Provenance rule

Heritage must be credible, not decorative.

- Replace vague words such as “quality” and “tradition” with concrete details about place, process, material and people.
- Use provenance only where it can be verified.
- Do not use “single-origin,” “organic,” “hand-harvested,” “direct trade,” “fair trade,” or community-impact claims unless the founder supplies proof and scope.
- If a detail is unknown, write around it honestly or mark it `[VERIFY BEFORE PUBLISHING]`.
- Specificity creates trust; exaggeration destroys it.

## Required output format

For each page, provide:

1. **Page title**
2. **SEO title** — maximum 60 characters
3. **Meta description** — maximum 155 characters
4. **Hero eyebrow**
5. **Hero headline**
6. **Hero subheadline**
7. **Primary button**
8. **Secondary link**, if appropriate
9. **Full page copy** — poetic, continuous storytelling with H2/H3 headings
10. **Narrative role** — explain in one line how the page moves the customer from curiosity to trust or action
11. **Visual direction** — describe 3–5 real photography or design moments
12. **Internal links** — collections, policies, contact, story pages
13. **Trust elements** — only realistic, verifiable trust signals

## Page objectives

### 1. About Us — sells the brand

**Objective:** introduce Graceylon and make the visitor feel the world of Ceylon.

Begin with a sensory or emotional image of Ceylon. Introduce Graceylon as more than a store: an experience shaped by Ceylon’s character.

Include:

- The meaning of the name Graceylon
- The brand promise
- Ceylon tea, spices and artisan creations as experiences
- The customer Graceylon serves
- Values: authenticity, provenance, craftsmanship, quiet luxury, respect for Ceylon
- A short quality promise
- CTA: “Discover Our Treasures”

**Length:** 400–550 words.

### 2. Our Story — creates emotion

**Objective:** tell the founder/brand journey as a personal, poetic narrative.

Begin with a moment, memory, scent, landscape or feeling connected to Ceylon. Move into the realisation that Ceylon is often experienced only from a distance or reduced to a generic product label. Describe the turning point: the decision to create Graceylon.

Include:

- The personal or cultural connection to Ceylon
- The inspiration behind the name Graceylon
- The gap between Ceylon’s richness and how its products are often presented
- The decision to create a brand centred on experience, provenance and quiet luxury
- How Graceylon curates or sources products
- The vision for the future
- A warm closing invitation

Use the customer-hero framework: the founder’s story should ultimately lead to the customer’s opportunity to experience Ceylon differently.

Use a continuous storytelling arc: setting, tension, turning point, solution, present day, future.

**Length:** 600–850 words.

### 3. Our Heritage — builds cultural value

**Objective:** help customers understand why Ceylon matters.

Write Ceylon’s heritage as a living story, not a history lesson.

Cover:

- Ceylon as the historic name for Sri Lanka
- Ceylon’s place in global spice and tea history
- Ceylon true cinnamon: origin, hand-rolled quills, sensory character, and distinction from cassia
- Ceylon tea: tea hills, elevation, climate, and international reputation
- Ceylon spices: cinnamon, pepper, cardamom, cloves, nutmeg and others as relevant
- Sri Lankan artisan traditions: natural materials, handcraft, texture, and the maker’s hand
- Why heritage matters to the modern customer

Use accurate general facts only. Do not invent dates, statistics, estate names, maker names, or supplier relationships.

**Length:** 800–1,100 words.

### 4. Sourcing & Quality — justifies the premium price

**Objective:** build trust through calm, credible, poetic transparency.

Begin with the idea that quality begins before the product reaches the customer: in soil, weather, harvest, hands, storage and care.

Cover:

- How Graceylon selects products
- What “authentic Ceylon” means for tea, spices, and artisan creations
- Origin and traceability approach
- Quality checks: appearance, aroma, taste, freshness, materials, construction, packaging and consistency
- Relationships with growers, makers, or trusted suppliers—phrase carefully and do not invent specifics
- Packaging and presentation standards
- What customers can expect when their order arrives
- A transparency statement explaining what Graceylon does and does not claim

The poetry should support the facts, not replace them.

**Length:** 600–900 words.

## Visual direction

Write image briefs that match the Graceylon visual world:

- Near-black backgrounds
- Warm ivory text
- Restrained gold accents
- Deep emerald greens
- Warm golden light
- Dark, moody, cinematic compositions
- Elegant serif headings
- Clean, readable body text
- Real product photography wherever possible

Do not describe AI-generated images as real estates, regions, makers, or products.

## Final instructions

- Produce original copy; do not copy existing brand websites.
- Make all pages feel like chapters of one cohesive Graceylon world.
- Ensure the copy appeals to customers in the UK, EU, US, and international markets.
- End every page with a relevant CTA.
- After generating all pages, add a section titled **“Claims and details to verify before publishing”** listing facts, certificates, supplier information, photos, product categories, delivery details and policies that require confirmation.
