Ancestral Provenance

Rooted in Ceylon. Alive in Sri Lanka.

“The name Ceylon carries the history of the island now called Sri Lanka. Its commercial tea story began in 1867, when James Taylor established a plantation at Loolecondera, near Kandy. Today it continues through estates and smallholder growers, pluckers, factory teams, blenders and tasters.”

Choosing Ceylon tea means choosing an origin with a story, and making it part of your day.

Perched in the central highlands above 6,000 feet, Nuwara Eliya’s misty peaks and crisp mountain breezes create microclimates unlike anywhere else on earth. Here, tea bushes grow slowly, drinking in pure alpine rain and solar flushes that enrich each tender bud with natural sweetness and high concentrations of restorative L-theanine.

While commercial industrialization pushed global tea production toward pulverized CTC dust, Sri Lanka guarded its orthodox heritage: hand-plucking only the tender unopened needle bud at daybreak, gentle withering on woven hessian tatts, and master hand-rolling that leaves cellular structures unbruised. Every infusion honors this unbroken chain of generational knowledge.

Botanical Truth & Craft

One island. Many teas.

Ceylon tea is defined by where it comes from, not by leaf size. It includes leafy and broken-leaf black teas, CTC teas, green teas and white teas, including silver and golden tips. Regions and processing give it a wide range, from fuller-bodied low-grown black teas to lighter, brighter high-grown ones.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Ceylon tea?

Tea grown and produced entirely within Sri Lanka, celebrated globally across black, green, and white styles from high, mid, and low elevations.

Is it always whole leaf?

No. Ceylon tea also includes broken-leaf orthodox grades and commercial CTC formats. Our Royal Silver Tips consist exclusively of 100% whole, unopened hand-plucked buds.

What does this tea taste like?

Delicate mountain florals, whispers of wild honey, and subtle white peach with a luminous amber-gold liquor and zero bitter astringency.

Where is it from?

Single-estate gardens in Nuwara Eliya, situated in Sri Lanka's misty central highlands above 6,000 feet elevation.

How do I brew it?

Steep 2.5g in 80°C–85°C water for 3–4 minutes. Because whole leaf cells remain intact, you can re-steep up to 3 times.
